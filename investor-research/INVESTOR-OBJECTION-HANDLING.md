It funds three things: first paying clinics (my time plus a part-time clinic-success contractor), patient demand generation in 3–5 launch metros, and the first engineering hire so I'm not the only builder. ## "What is your business model?"

> "Layered, and sequenced deliberately. The wedge is a free verified directory listing — zero-friction supply acquisition. Monetization layers on where clinics already see value: paid patient-acquisition campaigns (the landing page + assessment infrastructure is live), onboarding/profile services, hiring fees on the workforce marketplace, and vendor fees on the B2B marketplace. Longer-term, demand intelligence subscriptions. None of it is monetized yet — pricing gets set with the first 10 paying clinics rather than invented in a spreadsheet."

## "Who are your competitors?"

> "Three buckets. DTC telehealth — Hims, Ro, Maximus — they own consumer demand but structurally compete *against* independent clinics; we're the clinic-side counter-position. Practice software — Tebra, Healthie, med-spa platforms — they do operations but not demand generation or hiring. And marketing agencies, which rent leads without building infrastructure. Nobody I've found combines verified discovery, intake, hiring, and commerce in one clinic-facing platform for this vertical. That's an observation, not a moat claim — the moat has to be earned through clinic density and data."

## "Why are you the right founder?"

> "Two reasons, one demonstrated and one structural. Demonstrated: I designed, built, and shipped this entire ecosystem alone — four deployed applications, the database architecture, the security model, and the CRM I do outreach with. That's verifiable, not a resume claim. Structural: this business is won by shipping fast and talking to clinics constantly, and right now I do both personally — every clinic call feeds directly into product the same week. The round adds the commercial and engineering muscle around that loop."

## "Is this already live?"

> "Yes — and you can verify without me on the call. novalyte.io/directory is the live directory; ads.novalyte.io serves campaign landing pages with working embedded assessments; the workforce and marketplace routes are public on novalyte.io. What's *not* yet proven is production patient volume and revenue — I label those honestly on the portal as in progress."

## "How are you handling healthcare compliance?"

> "Architecturally, from day one, and with clear boundaries. Novalyte is a technology facilitator — clinics and licensed providers make all medical decisions; we never diagnose, prescribe, or treat, and the assessments are informational intake, not clinical diagnostics. Concretely: sensitive assessment responses stay in our secured database — never in analytics tools; we use row-level security so clinics can't see each other's data; consent language is versioned; documents in the investor data room are served through short-lived signed URLs with audit logs. Where we need formal review — marketing-compliance rules like anti-kickback considerations in lead routing, state-by-state telehealth nuances — that's budgeted for healthcare counsel in the use of funds. I'd rather show you the boundaries we've drawn than claim a compliance certification we don't have."

## "What specifically will the capital be used for?"

> "Three lines, roughly: ~45% first commercial motion — my full-time focus on clinic conversion, a part-time clinic-success contractor, and paid demand tests in 3–5 metros; ~35% first engineering hire so product velocity survives me spending half my time selling; ~20% healthcare counsel, infrastructure, and buffer. The milestone it buys: 100+ verified clinics live, 500+ patient assessments routed, and the first 3–5 paying clinics — which is the seed story."

## "Why are you fundraising before generating more traction?"

> "Fair challenge — the honest answer is speed and category timing, not need for validation. I could grind to first revenue solo; it would just take 12 months instead of 4, and the men's health category is being shaped right now by DTC players. The product risk is largely retired — you can click through everything. What's left is commercial execution, and that's a resourcing problem: my time is currently split between building and selling. Capital converts that split into parallel motion. If an investor wants to see first revenue before committing, I respect that — I'll keep them on the update list, and some of this round may close after those proof points land anyway."
